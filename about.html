Colophon · the making of

Print, not pixel-perfect.

Folio is deliberately static — a typeset page, not an app that phones home. This page explains who wrote what, why the architecture is what it is, and how the whole thing runs on a budget of zero.

I —

The zero-euro architecture

Current stack

  • Hosting: GitHub Pages. Unlimited, free, no card, CDN-served.
  • Frontend: vanilla HTML/CSS/JS, no build step, no tracker, no cookie banner.
  • Credentials: stay in the visitor’s browser. No backend to leak them.
  • Libs: pdf-lib & JSZip & sql.js, each from the official CDN.
  • Domain: user.github.io/… — or a free is-a.dev / eu.org subdomain later, via a CNAME file.

Proxy-backed reader (free)

The platforms we can’t reach in-browser are blocked by CORS. A cheap reverse proxy — Cloudflare Worker, 100k free requests/day, no card — can relay those APIs and push them through the same Reader UI.

The Reader already reads a PROXY constant in app/lib/common.js. Point it at your Worker and the “proxy-ready” badges light up. The Worker code ships in worker/.

Why not host the whole thing on a “free” PaaS? Most free tiers sleep, expire (Railway’s $5 credit, Render’s spin-downs) or demand a card. GitHub Pages + Cloudflare free tier do not.

III —

Legal, plainly

These tools make copies of books you already own or have licensed onto your own devices. That is lawful personal backup in most jurisdictions. Folio is a directory — it never touches your files, never uploads a page, and never redistributes anything.

Don’t publish the books you download. Don’t share tokens. Read the licence and disclaimer printed inside each tool folder — they predate Folio and remain yours to follow.
